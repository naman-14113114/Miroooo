Travel

Electric toothbrush travel guide.

Protect the head, prevent accidental activation and check the rules that apply to rechargeable devices before you fly.

Miroooo Editorial Team · Updated September 1, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick packing list

Charge the handle, let the head dry, pack it in a ventilated protective case, prevent the power button being pressed, bring the correct cable and check your airline’s current rules for battery-powered devices.

Before you leave

  1. Check the charge. Long battery life helps, but charge before a longer trip instead of relying on a stated maximum.
  2. Clean and dry the head. Rinse it after use and give it time to air-dry before closing the case.
  3. Pack the right cable. Check the handle’s charging connection and the plug or USB supply available at your destination.
  4. Protect the switch. Use a fitted case or a secure position in your bag so pressure cannot activate the handle.

Can an electric toothbrush go on a plane?

Rechargeable toothbrushes are battery-powered portable electronic devices. The US Transportation Security Administration (TSA) and Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) allow electric toothbrushes in both carry-on and checked bags, though lithium-battery devices are recommended in carry-on baggage. If placed in checked baggage, the handle should be completely switched off and protected from accidental activation. IATA passenger guidance specifically includes electric toothbrushes among everyday lithium-powered items and advises protecting them in a case or pouch in carry-on luggage.

Airline, route and local rules can differ and can change. Check the airline’s current baggage page before traveling; its instructions take priority over a general packing guide.

Keeping the brush head fresh

A sealed case is useful in transit, but do not leave a wet head closed for the entire trip. Let it air-dry upright when you arrive. Keep the bristles away from loose toiletries and the inside of a wash bag. If several people share a bathroom, keep individual heads separate and identifiable.

Do you need to bring the charger?

Compare the length of your trip with the charge actually left in your handle, not only the maximum battery figure on a product page. A brush used twice daily may last through a short trip without its charger, while an older battery, frequent mode changes or an incomplete starting charge can reduce the margin.
